# Product lens

Score: 4 | Vote: FUND IF

## What is strong

- A real design principle sits under the product: ask the user for as little as possible. It is already built into several features, not just written down: one sentence logs almost anything, one photo becomes a meal or a receipt, steps and bank transactions come in on their own, the app guesses and the user confirms, and Done/Snooze/+250 ml work straight from the notification.
- Craft in the parts nobody sees: an offline outbox, contrast and token tests, i18n, reminders that follow the user's local time through DST, and the same recurrence cases tested on both sides. That standard usually carries over into the parts people do see.
- Forgiving mechanics (freeze tokens, rest days, no failure states) suit the stated customer, a "lazy" person who quits the moment a streak breaks.
- The founder's own research already concludes "no paywall on any low-input entry path". That is the experience coming first and the monetisation fitted around it.

## What would kill it (the top 3, most dangerous first)

1. **No one thing.** Fifteen screens and twelve modules make a "life OS", and that does not fit in one sentence. A new user meets habits, money, health, food, notes, family, circle and an AI mentor all at once, and the promise of low input turns into high choice. Each module also goes up against a focused app that does only that job (the founder's own list names around twenty). When every module is second-best, the whole is a drawer of second-best tools and nobody has a reason to switch. Nothing has been cut yet. The research's "build next" list only adds.
2. **Seams where it hands off to systems the product does not own.** The core moment, a reminder arriving on time and acting on it from the notification, depends on a free host that sleeps (one reminder was already missed), OEM alarm windows (late on OnePlus, fix not yet tested), email routed through a third-party API, and an AI gateway that fails silently. For the customer, a reminder that arrives late *is* the product failing, and an AI feature that quietly turns dumb is a broken promise they cannot see the reason for. The seams sit right on the experience the pitch sells.
3. **The first five minutes are not designed as a single path.** A goal picker and starter packs exist, but Insights, the weekly review and the Outlook card only pay off after days of data. With no measured activation or day-7 retention, nobody knows whether a new user gets value in the first session or bounces off the breadth.

## The questions I would ask the founder

- In one sentence, with no list, what does Growth Buddy do for someone?
- If you could keep only three modules, which three, and what would you delete first?
- Walk me through minute one to minute five for a new user on the phone. What do they type, tap and see, and what do they get back before they close the app?
- Which one moment, if it worked perfectly every day, would make someone keep the app? Is that moment reliable today on the free host and on OEM phones?
- What happens to the customer, step by step, when the AI is down? Do they ever find out?
- How many people outside you use it daily, and what do they open first? (Retention and activation are unknown. A real test, or /founder-consumer as a first simulated read, would answer this.)
- Which single-purpose apps does each kept module have to beat, and on what? (/founder-competitors)

## If FUND IF: the conditions

1. **Cut to one thing.** Choose the single daily loop (for example: one sentence or one photo in, one useful nudge back) and the few modules that feed it. Hide or remove the rest from the first-run experience until the core loop keeps users. Write it in one sentence and make the first screen say it.
2. **Make the core moment reliable before adding anything.** Reminders and notification actions must arrive on time on a host that does not sleep, on the main Android OEMs, with the OnePlus fix confirmed on a real phone. Any AI fallback must be visible to the user, not silent.
3. **Design and measure the first five minutes.** One path from install to a first value moment within the first session, then real activation and day-7 retention numbers from at least a small group of outside users before any paid tier is defined. Pricing waits until /founder-consumer and /founder-competitors show which kept module someone would pay for.
