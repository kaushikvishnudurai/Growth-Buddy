# Monopoly lens
Score: 3/10 | Vote: FUND IF

## What is strong
- Real product depth fast: 15 screens, offline outbox, i18n, accessibility tests, built solo in about 4.5 months. Building ability is proven, so the open questions are about the market, not about whether the founder can ship.
- There is a candidate secret: "low input" as the organising rule, not "more features". Quick-add from one sentence, routing from one photo, notifications you can act on and forgiving streaks all serve that one idea. Most trackers ask for more input, not less.
- A possible local wedge. RBI Account Aggregator bank feeds, WhatsApp logging and an Indian founder point to a market (young Indian professionals) where the global players (Copilot Money, Monarch, YNAB, Cal AI) are weak or missing. A big share of a small market could start here.
- Seeds of network effects: Family meal planner and Growth Circle are multi-user. Today these are features. They could become what keeps users from leaving.

## What would kill it (the top 3, most dangerous first)
1. **No distribution.** No acquisition channel, no users and no budget. On this lens a product with no way to reach buyers is not a business. Nothing yet says how the first 100 users arrive or what each one costs.
2. **No 10x on any single axis, against a crowded field.** Each module goes up against a focused leader the founder has already benchmarked (Daylio, Finch, Habitica, MyFitnessPal, Cal AI, YNAB, Todoist and others), and phone makers bundle steps, sleep and reminders free. "All in one" improves on the incumbents bit by bit. It is not a breakthrough, and an AI-assistant company or a super-app could copy a bundle of trackers by month six.
3. **Durability depends on things the business does not control.** Free hosting that sleeps and has already missed a reminder, an AI gateway that silently fails, and OEM alarm windows on some phones all hit reliability, and reliability is the core promise of a reminder and habit product. Nothing proprietary (data, scale, brand) protects it yet.

## The questions I would ask the founder
- What is the small market you will own first? Name one segment and one module, for example "salaried 22-30 year olds in Indian metros tracking money via Account Aggregator". Do not answer with the whole life OS.
- What do you know that Daylio, Finch and the bank apps do not? Can you show it in one metric where you are 10x better, for example taps per day logged or days to first insight?
- How do the first 100 users arrive, and what does each cost? Is WhatsApp, the shareable story card or Family/Circle invites a real viral loop, and what is its invite rate?
- What is retention at day 30 for the users you have, even if that is 10 people? (No number exists. /founder-consumer can test demand before launch. Real retention needs a real cohort.)
- How big is the starting segment, who already sells to it and at what price? (Unknown. /founder-competitors answers this.)
- What does a paying user pay, what do they get, and does that cover hosting and AI costs per user once you leave the free tier? (Unknown. /founder-cfo answers this.)
- In ten years, what holds the position: the data history, the family/circle network, or the brand?

## If FUND IF: the conditions
1. Pick one wedge: one segment plus one or two modules that lead (the rest stays but is not the pitch), and size it with /founder-competitors.
2. Show one 10x claim with a measurement: for example logging effort or time to first insight against the best focused competitor in that wedge.
3. Name a distribution channel and run it: 100 real users from it, with cost per user and day-30 retention reported.
4. Make one network loop measurable (Family, Circle or the shared card): invites sent per active user.
5. Make reminders reliable before chasing growth: no reminders missed because hosting went to sleep.
