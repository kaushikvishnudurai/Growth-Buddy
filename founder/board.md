# Board: Growth Buddy

**Vote: 3 fund if, 0 fund, 0 pass. Average score 3.7 / 10** (Offers 4, Monopoly 3, Product 4).

Memos: [offers](board/offers.md), [monopoly](board/monopoly.md), [product](board/product.md).

The board backs the builder and the low-input principle. It does not yet back the business: no
single promise, no buyer, no price, no channel, no users.

## Risks more than one member raised

1. **No one thing (all three).** Fifteen screens and a module list are not an outcome. Each module
   meets a focused leader (Daylio, Finch, Cal AI, YNAB, Todoist...), and "all in one" is an
   incremental improvement over them, not a 10x one. Offers: a buyer cannot believe a vague
   outcome. Monopoly: no 10x on any axis. Product: breadth turns low input into high choice.
2. **Reliability sits on the core promise (all three).** A reminder app whose host sleeps, whose
   AI fails silently and whose reminders arrive late on OnePlus breaks the "you won't have to
   remember" promise, and rules out any guarantee.
3. **No proof (all three).** No outside users, no activation, retention or day-30 numbers, no
   testimonials.
4. **No market and no route to it (Offers, Monopoly).** The pain is real but rarely urgent, the
   target pays little for subscriptions, and nothing says how the first 100 users arrive or what
   they cost.

## Where the members disagree

- **What to lead with.** Offers and Monopoly both point at **Money for young Indian professionals**
  (bank feed via Account Aggregator, receipt scan, measurable outcome: rupees saved), a local wedge
  where global players are weak. Product does not name a module; it wants **the daily loop** (one
  sentence or photo in, one useful nudge back) as the product, whatever it logs.
- **What comes first.** Monopoly puts distribution first: no channel means no business. Product
  puts the first five minutes and reliability first, and wants pricing to wait. Offers wants the
  paid tier defined and priced early, so the first cohort can be a paying one.

## Conditions (the checklist for the rest of the pack)

- [ ] **One sentence, one buyer, one outcome.** Name one buyer (age, job, city, income) and one
      result with a number and a date. Rewrite the first screen and the pitch around it; the other
      modules become the stack behind it. (`/founder-consumer`, `/founder-offer`)
- [ ] **Pick the wedge and size it.** One segment plus one or two lead modules; who already sells
      to it and at what price. (`/founder-competitors`)
- [ ] **Hide the rest from first run.** Modules outside the wedge stay but are not in a new user's
      first session.
- [ ] **Show one 10x number** against the best focused competitor in the wedge: taps per day to
      log, or days to first insight.
- [ ] **Reliable core moment.** No reminder missed because the host slept (move off Render free or
      a dependable external pinger), the OnePlus fix confirmed on a real phone, AI fallback visible
      to the user, not silent.
- [ ] **Design and measure the first five minutes.** One path from install to a first value moment
      in session one.
- [ ] **Paid tier sells more outcome or less effort** (AI estimates, bank-feed automation, family /
      Circle seats), never the low-input free paths. Priced from buyer answers
      (`/founder-consumer` then `/founder-pricing`), margin checked against AI and hosting cost per
      user (`/founder-cfo`).
- [ ] **One channel, 100 real users** from it, with cost per user, day-7 and day-30 retention.
- [ ] **One measurable network loop** (Family, Circle or the share card): invites per active user.
- [ ] **A guarantee the CFO can afford** and honest urgency (founding-member price) once delivery
      is reliable. (`/founder-offer`, `/founder-cfo`)

## The strongest version the board can see

Not the life OS as pitched. The strongest version is **a low-effort money-and-habits coach for
salaried 22-30 year olds in Indian metros**: the bank feed and receipt scan fill in the month on
their own, one sentence or one WhatsApp message logs the rest, and one nudge a day says what to do
next, with a promise like "save a measurable amount within 90 days without keeping a spreadsheet".
Habits, health and food stay as the stack behind that promise; Family and Circle become the paid,
shareable layer that brings in the next user. This narrows what was pitched on purpose. The Product
lens would accept any single loop the founder can make reliable and loved; Money is the board's
majority pick because it has the most measurable outcome and the clearest local edge.
